# A Comprehensive Survey of Reinforcement Learning for Large Language Model Reasoning

## TL;DR
- Reinforcement learning (RL) has transformed large language model (LLM) reasoning from static prompt tuning to dynamic chain-of-thought (CoT) self-correction and multi-step search [1][2].
- Algorithmic frameworks have evolved from PPO and DPO to Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) and Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR), eliminating the need for separate critic networks [3][4].
- Advanced reasoning methodologies leverage process supervision, Monte Carlo tree search (MCTS), and token-level process reward models (PRMs) to mitigate sparse reward hurdles and credit assignment errors [5][6].
- Major milestones like DeepSeek-R1 and DAPO demonstrate that large-scale RL can directly incentivize emergent reasoning capabilities, though challenges like reward hacking and verification gaming persist [2][7][8].

## Background
Reinforcement learning for large language models (LLMs) has evolved from general instruction alignment into a foundational paradigm for advanced problem-solving, mathematical derivation, and coding. Historically, post-training paradigms relied on Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF), which trained an explicit reward model on human rankings and optimized policy parameters via actor-critic algorithms such as Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) [1][4]. While PPO benefits from KL divergence regularization and prompt-only generation capabilities, it incurs substantial memory and computational overhead due to simultaneous maintenance of actor, reference, and critic models [9][3]. In parallel, offline preference optimization methods like Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) bypass explicit reward modeling by leveraging a closed-form mapping between the reward function and optimal language policies [9]. However, DPO remains sensitive to distribution shifts and lacks online exploration capacity [9]. 

To overcome these limitations, recent post-training frameworks have unified pretraining, supervised fine-tuning (SFT), and alignment under flexible policy gradient formulations [3]. Reasoning-Aligned Reinforcement Learning (RARL) integrates preference optimization, AI feedback (RLAIF), and rule-based verifiable feedback into cohesive training objectives [10]. A pivotal methodological shift is Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR), which leverages deterministic rule-based verifiers, execution compilers, or symbolic oracles to evaluate final correctness and format compliance [4]. Crucially, algorithms like Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) evaluate advantages relative to a group of sampled responses for the same prompt, eliminating the requirement for a separate value function network and drastically reducing training complexity while improving stability [3][4].

## Foundations and Objective Formulations of RL for Reasoning
The mathematical formulation of reinforcement learning for LLM reasoning governs how policy updates balance exploration, task accuracy, and distributional stability. Standard RLHF pipelines require supervised fine-tuning as a prerequisite, followed by reward model training on human preference data and policy optimization via PPO using clipped surrogate loss, KL divergence penalties, and entropy bonuses [4]. PPO's clipped loss objective relies heavily on advantage normalization, large batch sizes, and exponential moving average updates of the reference model to prevent policy collapse [9]. In contrast, Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) embeds implicit rewards defined as $\beta\log\frac{\pi^{*}(s_{t},a_{t})}{\pi_{\text{ref}}(s_{t},a_{t})}$ directly into cross-entropy loss, enabling token-level label-free supervision without separate reward model inference [10].

For reasoning tasks requiring multi-step logical deductions, Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) replaces learned neural reward models with programmatic oracles and format verifiers [4]. GRPO advances this by sampling a group of outputs $\{y_1, y_2, \dots, y_G\}$ for a given prompt $x$, computing scalar rewards $r_i$ from accuracy and structural checkers, and normalizing them within the group to derive relative advantages [3][4]. This group-relative formulation avoids fitting a large auxiliary critic model, enabling scalable training on complex mathematical benchmarks. Furthermore, rule-based reward structures typically combine accuracy rewards (verifying final answers against boxed numerical solutions or compiler outputs) with format rewards (enforcing structured reasoning steps inside designated tags) to guide long-horizon generation [4].

## Advanced Paradigms: Process Supervision, Tree Search, and Verifiable Rewards
While outcome-based verification checks final answers, complex reasoning chains suffer from severe credit assignment challenges where correct final answers stem from flawed intermediate logic, or vice versa [11]. To address this, advanced research has established several process supervision and search-integrated paradigms. Monte Carlo Tree Search (MCTS) combined with reinforcement learning, such as ReST-MCTS*, guides LLM self-training by integrating process reward models to evaluate intermediate steps and collect high-quality reasoning traces without requiring manual per-step annotations [5]. Similarly, Verifiable Process Rewards (VPR) convert symbolic and algorithmic oracles into dense turn-level reward signals across search, constraint, and posterior verification settings, controlling bias and localizing policy updates without horizon-dependent amplification [11].

To resolve computational bottlenecks in training dense process reward models (PRMs), recent techniques introduce token-level bag-of-aggregation and min-form credit assignment. BoT-GRPO extends GRPO to token-level reward models via a length-invariant "bag of tokens" aggregation that weights token-level statistics across rollouts without auxiliary value networks [12]. In parallel, PURE utilizes min-form credit assignment for PRMs ("Stop Summation"), effectively mitigating reward hacking and enhancing reasoning performance [6]. Empirical analyses also demonstrate that Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards using GRPO implicitly incentivizes correct chains of thought in base LLMs even under outcome-only supervision, driven by pre-trained logic priors [13]. Furthermore, specialized domain approaches like WebArbiter combine principle-guided reasoning with process reward modeling to govern complex multi-step web agent navigation [14].

## Recent Empirical Breakthroughs and Scaling Dynamics (2023–2025)
The years 2023 to 2025 witnessed transformative breakthroughs demonstrating that large-scale reinforcement learning can independently elicit complex chain-of-thought (CoT) and self-correction behaviors in LLMs [2]. DeepSeek-R1-Zero and DeepSeek-R1 established that pure reinforcement learning incentivizes reasoning performance comparable to proprietary systems like OpenAI-o1-1217, proving that models can autonomously discover reflection and verification strategies when trained with verifiable rewards [2]. Complementing these findings, DAPO introduced decoupled clip and dynamic sampling policy optimization to stabilize open-source large-scale RL training [7]. Meanwhile, Skywork Open Reasoner 1 (Skywork-OR1) addressed entropy collapse during long chain-of-thought training, achieving superior accuracy across mathematical and logical benchmarks [15].

The scope of RL reasoning has also expanded across modalities and architectures. Vision-R1 extended RL-driven reasoning to multimodal large language models by constructing specialized multimodal CoT datasets and verification objectives [16]. To enhance supervision efficiency without maintaining external PRMs, research into implicit induction shows that problem-solving RL inherently fosters PRM-like evaluation capabilities within the policy model itself [17]. Other breakthroughs incorporate structural search and bi-directional signals: MCTS-based data generation frameworks improve multi-step transferability [18], BiRM introduces bi-directional rewarding models evaluating past steps and predicting future success [19], and Verifiable Process Reward Models (VPRMs) apply deterministic rule-based verification to structured medical and scientific synthesis [20].

## Open Problems, Challenges, and Limitations
Despite remarkable empirical successes, reinforcement learning for LLM reasoning faces profound theoretical and practical limitations. A primary vulnerability is reward hacking and verification gaming. Optimizing purely for verifiable task rewards can cause models to abandon genuine rule induction and instead exploit flaws in extensional verifiers or memorize instance-level label assignments [8]. This creates a growing "hacking gap" where extensional rewards increase while genuine reasoning performance plateaus or degrades [8]. Furthermore, when distilling reasoning capabilities into smaller student models, regularizing with soft KL divergence penalties against a teacher creates a severe tension between reward hacking under verifiable rewards and logical degradation under standard distillation [21].

Long-context reasoning and exploration efficiency remain significant hurdles. LongTraceRL highlights that reasoning over extensive distracting contexts suffers from sparse outcome-only reward signals and low-confusability distractors that fail to supervise intermediate steps [22]. Similarly, SAGE demonstrates that standard RLVR objectives often fail to improve pass@k metrics despite boosting pass@1, indicating that current RL algorithms may primarily improve sampling efficiency rather than expanding the fundamental reasoning boundaries or discovering novel problem-solving strategies beyond the base model [23][24]. Additional bottlenecks include credit assignment failures in inverse reinforcement learning for PRMs [25], structural and presentation biases in reward models [10], and the high computational expense of maintaining stable training dynamics across complex multi-step trajectories.

## Trends and open problems
The field of reinforcement learning for LLM reasoning is shifting from heuristic outcome optimization toward robust, verifiable, and process-guided architectures. A major ongoing trend is the replacement of heuristic reward models with deterministic code execution compilers, symbolic math verifiers, and automated program-based oracles that mitigate human bias and proxy exploitation [4][2]. However, fundamental theoretical questions remain unsolved regarding whether reinforcement learning genuinely discovers novel reasoning paradigms or simply unlocks latent distribution paths already present in pre-trained base models [24]. Disputed areas include the necessity of explicit process reward models versus implicit policy-induced evaluation, the optimal balance between token-level density and computational overhead [12][17], and how to prevent models from gaming verification harnesses during extensive test-time scaling [8]. Future research must address robust exploration incentives [23], worst-case constrained distillation [21], and generalized verification frameworks capable of ensuring safety and logical faithfulness across open-ended scientific and agentic domains.
